Why ownership, not branding

A "Truly Canadian" storefront can be owned by a US private equity firm. An "iconic Canadian brand" can have been owned by an American company for over a decade. Names, flags, and marketing copy tell you what a company wants you to believe. Corporate filings and acquisition records tell you who actually gets the money. We research the second kind of fact — and we say plainly when we're not sure yet.

Now available · early version

The browser extension

Checks who owns a shop while you shop. Visit a retailer we've researched and a small badge tells you who owns it, without you having to look anything up. Found something we've got wrong? Report it from the badge in a couple of clicks.

This is an early version, and it improves on its own. Your browser updates extensions quietly in the background, so the copy you install today becomes the better one without you reinstalling anything or checking back. A bigger release is close behind this one: it refreshes the company list every few hours, so newly researched companies and corrected entries reach you the same day instead of waiting on a store review.

Which means if something is missing or wrong right now, the useful thing is to tell us rather than uninstall. Every report goes to a person, most gaps are a company we haven't researched yet, and the fix shows up in your browser on its own.

Add to Chrome — free

Works in Chrome, and in Edge, Brave, Opera and Vivaldi, which all install from the Chrome Web Store. A Firefox build is finished and waiting on review at addons.mozilla.org.

It draws on the same research as the directory here, so nothing in the extension is information you can't already search on this site.

Get a company added, or tell us we got something wrong

MapleCheck is researched by hand, one company at a time, using public filings, corporate registries, and acquisition records — not automated guessing. If you run a Canadian company we haven't covered yet, or you've spotted something inaccurate, this is how to reach us.

What to send us

The one thing that matters most is how you know. A filing, a news article, a statement from the company, or first-hand knowledge all work — but without it we can't verify anything, and unverified entries stay flagged.

  • The company name and website
  • Who you believe owns it, and how you know — a registry filing, an acquisition report, a note from an owner or officer, or direct knowledge
  • Anything that speeds up verification: province of incorporation, parent company, recent sale or acquisition
  • If you know it: where the products are actually made, and where the materials come from

That last point is a separate question from ownership, and we track it separately. A company can be Canadian-owned and manufacture entirely overseas — plenty in our directory do.

How we handle what you send

Every entry is held to the same bar regardless of who submits it: public filings and corporate registries first, deal and acquisition records for private companies, and a clear "still being verified" label when sources genuinely disagree or a sale is in progress.

Self-reported ownership from someone who works at or runs the company counts as a strong source — not an automatic pass. If you tell us your own company is Canadian-owned, we'll say so and note that it came from you.

We'd rather show a company as unverified than guess. If we can't confirm something, the entry says exactly that.

Something looks wrong

If we've got an entry wrong — the ownership, the country, or a detail that's gone out of date because a company was sold — tell us. Ownership changes more often than people expect, and stale entries are the failure we most want to catch.

Use the form below, or email maplecheck@northmail.ca with what should change and, if you have one, a source. Once the browser extension is released you'll also be able to report an error directly from the page you're looking at.
